## Dual Classing

Single-classed Fighters, Magic-Users, Clerics, and Thieves can change character class, progressing in the new class and abandoning the old one. The only limitation is that Magic-Users may not switch to become Clerics, nor can Clerics switch to Magic-User. In order to change class, the character must have a minimum attribute score of 16 in the Prime Attribute of the new class.

When a character switches classes, XP earned in the new class is divided by the level of the former class until the new class reaches that level. Example: Frog the magic-user is level 4 and is dual-classing into thief. His XP is divided by 4 until he reaches level 4 as a thief.

## Multiclassing

•             Dwarves: Fighter/Thief
•             Elves: Fighter/Magic-User, Fighter/Magic-User/Thief
•             Half-Elves: Fighter/Magic-User, Fighter/Magic-User/Cleric
## Hit Points
Hit Points are **not rolled**. Characters begin with a fixed amount based on **Ancestry, Constitution, and Class**.
## Level 1 HP
**Starting HP = CON + Ancestry HP + CON Bonus + Class Bonus**

**CON Bonus:** +1 HP for every point of CON above 12.

**Class Bonuses**
• Fighter-types: **+5 HP**
• Thief-types: **+4 HP**
• Cleric-types: **+3 HP**
• Magic-User types: **+0 HP**

For multiclass characters use **+3 Fighter, +2 Thief, +1 Cleric** instead of the normal class bonus.

**Blooded Regents:** +10 HP per the Birthright Rulebook.

## Early Levels
The +1 HP at levels **2, 3, and 4** is a **character benefit**, not a class benefit. It is gained only once, regardless of multiclassing or later dual-classing.

## HP After 4th Level
⚔️ Fighter & Thief types: **+1 HP/level**
✝️ Cleric types: **+1 HP/2 levels**
🔮 Magic-User types: **+1 HP/3 levels**

### Multiclass
Use the **most favorable HP advancement rate** among the character's classes. HP gains do **not stack**.

Allowed combinations:
• Dwarf — Fighter/Thief
• Half-Elf — Fighter/Magic-User or Fighter/Magic-User/Cleric
• Elf — Fighter/Magic-User or Fighter/Magic-User/Thief

Since all allowed combinations include Fighter, they gain **+1 HP per level after 4th**.

### Dual-Class
When a character stops advancing in one class and begins another:
• Keep all existing HP.
• Do **not** gain the level 2–4 bonuses again.
• Do **not** gain another starting class HP bonus.
• Thereafter, use the **new active class's** HP advancement rate.

Example: Fighter 6 → Magic-User keeps all current HP, then gains **+1 HP per 3 Magic-User levels**.

## Example
**Human (M) Fighter, CON 14**
14 CON +14 Human +2 CON Bonus +5 Fighter = **35 HP at level 1**
Levels 2–4 add +1 each = **38 HP at level 4**.

# Overland Movement

*Campaign House Rule*

## Daily Travel on Foot

The party travels at the pace of its **slowest member**. Use each traveler’s movement rate after accounting for carried weight.

Overland travel uses these distance bands:

**12 → 9 → 6 → 3 → 2 → 1 mile per day**

The **2-mile and 1-mile bands apply only to overland travel**. They do not change combat or dungeon movement rates.

## Terrain Adjustments

Starting with the party’s normal movement band, reduce it according to the terrain:

| Terrain | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Maintained roads or open plains | Normal band |
| Light woods or hills | Down 1 band |
| Dense forest, rough hills, or sandy desert | Down 2 bands |
| Mountains or swamps | Down 3 bands |

Terrain alone cannot reduce daily travel below **1 mile**. Exploring, searching, and other delays can reduce progress further.

### Daily Distance by Terrain

| Terrain | Move 12 | Move 9 | Move 6 | Move 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Maintained roads or open plains | 12 miles | 9 miles | 6 miles | 3 miles |
| Light woods or hills | 9 miles | 6 miles | 3 miles | 2 miles |
| Dense forest, rough hills, or sandy desert | 6 miles | 3 miles | 2 miles | 1 mile |
| Mountains or swamps | 3 miles | 2 miles | 1 mile | 1 mile |

Distances represent **one day of normal travel on foot**.

## Travel Activities

Apply these adjustments after determining the terrain-adjusted distance.

| Activity | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Normal travel | Use the listed distance |
| Exploring and mapping | Half the listed distance |
| Thoroughly searching an area | Usually one-tenth the listed distance |
| Forced march | Double the listed distance, subject to Strength checks |

### Exploring and Mapping

Observing and mapping unfamiliar surroundings halves daily travel distance. This does not include thoroughly searching the entire area.

### Thorough Searching

Carefully searching an area usually reduces daily distance to **one-tenth normal**. The referee may adjust this according to the area and what the party is seeking.

### Forced March

A forced march doubles the party’s terrain-adjusted daily distance.

Each traveler makes a **daily Strength check**:

> Roll **1d20 equal to or below Strength** to succeed.

A traveler who fails cannot maintain the forced-march pace. If the party stays together, it must return to its normal travel pace for that day.

## Roads and Travel Conditions

A maintained road through woodland may allow the road rate. A steep mountain trail still uses the appropriate difficult-terrain rate.

Weather, damaged roads, river crossings, and other obstacles may cause additional delays or make a route impassable.

## Examples

A party with a slowest movement rate of **9** traveling through **dense forest** drops two bands:

**9 → 6 → 3 miles per day**

If exploring and mapping, that party travels **1½ miles per day**.

A heavily burdened party at **Move 3** entering **light woods** drops one band:

**3 → 2 miles per day**
## Regent Characters
A regent is a blooded character who rules a domain. A domain may be a landed realm, guild network, temple hierarchy, source network, law holding, or other recognized seat of power. A character who begins play as a regent must be blooded and must choose or create a domain with the Referee’s approval.

Regent characters receive the following benefits at character creation:

1.           Domain: The character begins with a domain appropriate to their class, bloodline, culture, and campaign role.
2.           Bonus Hit Points: The character gains +10 hit points. These hit points are added permanently while the character remains a regent. If the character abdicates, loses the domain, or is otherwise removed from rulership, these bonus hit points are lost.
3.           Regent Starting Resources: The character chooses one of the following:
- roll for one random magical item usable by the character’s class, subject to Referee approval; or
- begin play with a mount and any nonmagical armor and weapons the character may normally use.
These benefits represent the resources, training, household support, inheritance, and symbolic power available to a ruler. A regent is not merely an adventurer with a title; they are a person of consequence with obligations, enemies, dependents, and access to wealth and influence beyond that of ordinary beginning characters.

## Bloodlines and Regency
Divine inheritance, blood abilities, regency, and power to rule
In Cerilia, kings do not rule merely because they possess armies or wealth. They rule because divine blood flows through their veins.
### The Legacy of Deismaar
At the Battle of Mount Deismaar, the old gods confronted the dark god Azrai in a final cataclysmic struggle. When the gods perished, their divine essence exploded outward into the mortals closets to them.
Those touched by this power became the first blooded scions. Their descendants inherited fragments of divine might that persist to this day through bloodlines.
Bloodlines are among the most important forces shaping Cerilia. They determine legitimacy, rulership, inheritance, and supernatural power throughout the continent.
### What is a Scion?
A scion is a blooded individual carrying divine essence inherited from one of the gods who died at Deismaar.
## Bloodline Strength
Not all bloodlines possess equal power. Some are faint traces of ancient divinity, while others rival the legendary dynasties of Cerilia. Use the tables below to roll for your character's bloodline strength and derivation:

## Bloodline Derivations
Every bloodline descends from one of the gods who perished at Deismaar. This divine ancestry is called a derivation
### Anduiras
Bloodlines of rulership, nobility, leadership, courage, and warfare. Common among Anuirean noble dynasties and military rulers.
### Azrai
Bloodlines tied to corruption, shadow, fear, manipulation, and monstrous transformation. many awnsheghlien descend from Azrai's blood.
### Basaïa
Bloodlines associated with reason, brilliance, radiance, leadership, and enlightenment. Common among Khinasi dynasties and scholars.
### Brenna
Bloodlines of luck, trade, cunning, speed, stealth, and fortune. Frequently found among guilders, merchants, thieves, and adventurers.
### Masela
Bloodlines tied to the sea, endurance, adaptability, travel, and survival. Common among sailors, explorers, and coastal rulers.
### Reynir
Bloodlines connected to nature, wilderness, survival, beasts, and ancient natural power. Common among Rjurik clans and druids.
### Vorynn
Bloodlines of magic, knowledge, moonlight, mystery, and arcane mastery. Frequently found among wizards and scholars.

# Blood Abilities — Swords & Wizardry Conversion
### Blood Ability Ranks

When a blood ability is enhanced from Minor to Major or from Major to Great, it becomes the higher-ranked form of that same ability.

Whether lower-ranked effects are retained depends on the ability's description.

- If the higher rank says it extends, adds to, or includes the lower effect, the benefits are cumulative.
- If the higher rank improves a numerical value or replaces the earlier mechanic, use only the higher value.
- If the description is unclear, the Referee should interpret the higher rank as an enhancement of the same ability rather than as a separate power.

## Alertness

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** Basaiä, Brenna, Reynir, Azrai

The character possesses an uncanny awareness of his surroundings.

The character is surprised only on a roll of **1 on 1d6**.

Even in circumstances where the Referee would normally rule that surprise is automatic, the character may still roll for surprise normally.

---

## Alter Appearance

**Strength:** Major  
**Derivations:** Brenna, Vorynn, Azrai

The scion can briefly alter his apparent build and facial features through an innate illusion.

Once per day, the scion may alter his appearance as though using **Change Self** as a 5th-level Magic-User.

The effect lasts **1 turn + 1 round per character level**.

The scion may imitate the appearance of a particular individual if he has previously met that person.

## Animal Affinity

**Strength:** Minor, Major, Great  
**Derivations:** All

Each of the old gods was associated with a sacred animal. A scion with this ability possesses a supernatural affinity with the totem animal of his derivation.

| Derivation | Totem Animal |
|---|---|
| Anduiras | Lion |
| Basaiä | Eagle |
| Brenna | Cat |
| Masela | Dolphin |
| Reynir | Wolf |
| Vorynn | Owl |
| Azrai | Serpent |

### Minor

The scion can communicate empathically with a totem animal within **60 feet**, provided the scion and animal can see one another.

Natural members of the species will not attack the scion unless magically controlled, enraged by extraordinary circumstances, or otherwise compelled by the Referee.

### Major

The scion gains the Minor ability.

In addition, the scion may **speak with animals** of the totem species at will while they are within **10 feet**.

Such animals instinctively regard the scion as an ally and will cooperate with reasonable requests.

They may guide, warn, scout, or guard for the scion, but normally will not abandon their natural habitat or undertake obviously suicidal commands.

### Great

The scion gains the Minor and Major abilities.

The scion can sense the nearest totem animal within **1 mile**.

By concentrating for one round, the scion may do one of the following:

- See through the animal's eyes.
- Communicate mentally with it.
- Summon it to his location.

The scion may continue concentrating to locate other members of the species, one animal per round.

Totem animals are extraordinarily loyal to a scion possessing this power and may risk their lives in his defense.

Once per day, the scion may also **Shape Change** into the totem animal for up to **2 hours**.

## Battlewise

**Strength:** Major  
**Derivations:** Anduiras, Azrai

The scion possesses an instinctive understanding of warfare and command.

Any army personally commanded by the scion gains:

- **+1 Attack**
- **+1 Defense**

The scion must be physically present at the battle and personally commanding the force.

If opposing commanders both possess Battlewise, the bonuses cancel one another.

## Blood History

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** Brenna, Masela, Vorynn

The scion carries fragments of the memories of his direct ancestors.

By concentrating, the scion may attempt to recall information known by an ancestor whose memories form part of his bloodline.

The inherited memories extend only to the birth of the ancestor's direct heir.

For example, the scion possesses his father's memories only up to the scion's own birth, and his grandfather's memories only up to the birth of the scion's father.

The Referee determines what information is available and how clearly it can be recalled.

Blood History can reveal forgotten people, places, customs, events, and other ancestral knowledge, but it does not grant general omniscience.

## Bloodmark

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** Any

The scion bears an obvious and recognizable sign of divine ancestry.

Examples include:

- An unusual streak of hair.
- Brilliant or unnatural eyes.
- A distinctive birthmark.
- Another hereditary physical trait.

Those who recognize the mark instinctively understand that the character is a scion of consequence.

When the Bloodmark would reasonably influence an NPC, the scion gains **+1 to +4 on reaction rolls**, depending upon the fame of the bloodline and the circumstances.

The Referee determines the exact bonus.

A Bloodmark does not automatically make enemies friendly; it may instead cause fear, caution, deference, or hostility depending upon the situation.

## Character Reading

**Strength:** Major  
**Derivations:** Basaiä, Brenna, Vorynn

The scion is extraordinarily skilled at reading the intentions and motives of others.

After speaking with a creature for at least **1 round**, the scion may attempt to determine its general intentions, attitude, and sincerity.

The scion has a **90% chance** to recognize deliberate treachery, deception, or a bad-faith bargain.

This ability does not reveal alignment.

If a creature's intentions are concealed by magic, the blood ability provides no automatic means of penetrating the magical effect unless the Referee rules otherwise.

## Courage

**Strength:** Minor, Major, Great  
**Derivation:** Anduiras

### Minor

The scion is immune to normal fear.

Against magical fear, the scion automatically succeeds on any saving throw normally allowed.

If the effect normally permits **no saving throw**, the scion receives a saving throw with a **-2 penalty**.

### Major

The Minor ability extends to all allies within **10 feet** of the scion.

### Great

The scion gains the Minor and Major abilities.

In mass combat, troops personally commanded by the scion automatically succeed on **Morale checks** caused by fear or intimidation.

## Detect Lie

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** Any except Azrai

Once per day, the scion may concentrate upon one creature and determine whether it knowingly speaks falsehoods.

This functions as **Detect Lie** for **1 turn**.

The ability does not reveal hidden motives, evasions, mistakes, or incomplete knowledge; it detects deliberate falsehoods.

---

## Detect Illusion

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** Any except Anduiras

Whenever the scion encounters a visual illusion, he immediately receives a **saving throw** to recognize it as false, even if he was not actively attempting to disbelieve it.

If the scion deliberately examines or tests the illusion, he gains a **+4 bonus** to the saving throw.

This ability applies to effects that create or substitute false visual images.

It does not provide special protection against effects such as invisibility, blindness, fear, or other magic that does not create a false image.

---

## Direction Sense

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** Masela, Reynir

The scion possesses an instinctive sense of direction.

As long as he knows his destination, he always knows its general direction and can retrace a route he has previously traveled.

The ability does not reveal the safest or easiest path and does not allow passage through impassable terrain.

### Masela

The scion cannot become naturally lost while traveling at sea or along major bodies of water.

### Reynir

The ability functions only in wilderness and rural terrain.

## Divine Aura

**Strength:** Major, Great  
**Derivations:** All except Brenna

The scion radiates an unmistakable aura of supernatural authority.

When the aura is relevant to an encounter, the scion gains **+1 to +4 on reaction rolls**, as determined by the Referee.

Once per day, the scion may intensify this aura by concentrating for one round.

The heightened aura lasts **1 round per level**.

### Major

All nonblooded creatures of **4+1 Hit Dice or fewer** within **30 feet** must make a saving throw or become enthralled by the scion for the duration.

Enthralled creatures cease hostile actions and give the scion their full attention but are not mindless slaves.

### Great

The scion gains the Major ability.

When the aura is intensified:

- Nonhostile creatures of 4+1 HD or fewer are affected as though by **Hypnotic Pattern**.
- Hostile creatures of 4+1 HD or fewer must make a saving throw or suffer the effects of **Fear**.

Both effects may function simultaneously.

## Divine Wrath

**Strength:** Major  
**Derivations:** Anduiras, Basaiä, Masela

When grievously wronged or when confronting one who has harmed the scion or someone deeply important to him, the scion may be overcome by divine fury.

Divine Wrath **cannot be activated voluntarily**. The Referee determines when circumstances and roleplaying warrant its manifestation.

While the wrath lasts, the scion gains:

- **+3 to attack rolls**
- **+3 to damage**
- **One additional melee attack each round**
- **+3 on all saving throws**
- Damage from successful attacks is reduced by **1 point per damage die**

Any spell cast by the scion that has a variable numerical effect produces the **maximum possible result**.

Any enemy within **10 feet** who meets the scion's gaze must make a saving throw or flee in fear for **2d6 rounds**.

Divine Wrath lasts for **1 turn + 1 round per level**, or until the battle or confrontation ends.

## Elemental Control

**Strength:** Great  
**Derivations:** Anduiras, Basaiä, Masela, Reynir

The scion possesses a supernatural connection to the element associated with his divine ancestor.

Once per week, the scion may summon an elemental of the appropriate type.

The elemental obeys the scion without requiring concentration and never turns upon him.

It remains for **1 turn**, then returns to its place of origin.

The scion also gains the following derivation-specific power:

| Derivation | Element | Additional Power |
|---|---|---|
| Anduiras | Air | **Gust of Wind**, 3/day |
| Basaiä | Fire | Constant **Resist Fire** |
| Masela | Water | **Water Walk** for up to 3 turns/day |
| Reynir | Earth | **Meld into Stone** or **Passwall**, 1/day |

## Enhanced Sense

**Strength:** Minor, Major  
**Derivations:** All

### Anduiras

**Minor:** Detect Evil up to **3 times per day**.

**Major:** Detect Evil **at will**.

### Azrai

**Minor:** The scion sees normally in dim light and shadow. Creatures attempting to Hide in Shadows while within the scion's sight have their chance of success **halved**.

**Major:** The scion may perceive traces of the Shadow World.

By concentrating for one round, the scion has a **75% chance** to detect:

- Evil.
- Undead.
- Necromantic magic.

The scion may attempt this as often as desired.

He may also recognize places where the barrier between Cerilia and the Shadow World is unusually thin.

### Basaiä

**Minor:** The scion possesses extraordinary distance vision and can distinguish detail at roughly ten times normal range.

Missile attacks are treated as **one range category closer** for purposes of range penalties.

**Major:** Once per day, the scion can see through normal and magical darkness to **60 feet** for **1 turn**.

### Brenna

The scion gains **30-foot infravision**.

When the scion's side rolls for surprise, improve the party's chance to avoid surprise by **1**, provided the scion is able to perceive the approaching danger.

There is no Major version.

### Masela

**Minor:** Weather does not impair the scion's ordinary vision or hearing. Fog, rain, snow, wind, and similar natural conditions do not reduce his effective sight below **120 feet** unless darkness or some other obstruction intervenes.

**Major:** If the scion's name is spoken outdoors within **10 miles**, and the scion is also outdoors, he hears his name and the surrounding conversation carried upon the wind.

### Reynir

The scion can track in the wilderness as a Ranger of equal level.

If already a Ranger, the Referee should treat the scion as exceptionally skilled and grant an appropriate advantage when tracking.

In addition, while in the wilderness the scion is surprised only on a roll of **1 on 1d6**.

There is no Major version.

### Vorynn

**Minor:** Once per day, after concentrating for one round, the scion may receive a supernatural omen concerning the likely result of a proposed action.

The information should be comparable to a powerful divination: useful but not perfectly complete.

**Major:** In addition, the power may activate automatically when the scion unknowingly approaches serious danger.

There is normally a **60% chance** that the warning occurs, modified by the Referee according to the severity and supernatural significance of the danger.

**Derivation:** Azrai

A successful touch attack forces the target to make a saving throw or suffer the effects of **Fear** as though cast by a 10th-level Magic-User.

The scion gains **+2 on saving throws against fear**.

**2d6 + twice the scion's level**

## Poison Sense

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** Azrai, Reynir

The scion instinctively senses poison and other harmful substances.

He has:

- **90% chance** to detect poison within 3 feet.
- **25% chance** to detect poison within 10 feet.

The Referee makes this check secretly.

The ability is passive and requires no conscious search.

It warns the scion that something is dangerous but does not necessarily identify the exact poison.

## Protection from Evil

**Strength:** Major  
**Derivations:** All except Azrai

The scion is constantly surrounded by an aura equivalent to **Protection from Evil, 10-foot Radius**.

Evil creatures suffer **-1 on attack rolls** against the scion.

The scion cannot be magically charmed, possessed, or dominated by an evil creature unless he willingly permits the effect.

A Paladin with this blood ability increases the radius to **15 feet**.

## Regeneration

**Strength:** Great  
**Derivations:** Anduiras, Reynir, Azrai

The scion heals **1 hit point per hour**.

Given sufficient time, the scion can recover from permanent physical injuries that would normally leave lasting scars or disabilities, including:

- Severe burns.
- Crippling injuries.
- Physical blindness.
- Lameness.
- Permanent scarring.

The scion cannot regrow a severed body part.

Regeneration cannot remove a magical curse, paralysis, magical blindness, or another ongoing magical condition.

For serious permanent injuries, the scion recovers approximately **1% of the lost function per day**, reaching full recovery after roughly 100 days.

If the campaign uses rules allowing characters to remain alive below 0 hit points, Regeneration prevents further automatic hit-point loss while the scion remains alive.

## Resistance

**Strength:** Minor, Major, Great  
**Derivations:** All

The scion possesses supernatural resistance to a particular class of danger.

| Strength | Chance to Resist |
|---|---:|
| Minor | 25% |
| Major | 50% |
| Great | 75% |

Whenever the scion is affected by the appropriate type of magic or hazard, roll the resistance chance **before making any saving throw**.

If the resistance succeeds, the effect is negated unless the derivation description says otherwise.

### Anduiras

Resistance applies to magical charm, enchantment, domination, and similar mind-controlling magic.

### Azrai

Resistance applies to necromantic magic and energy- or level-draining attacks.

### Basaiä

Resistance applies to fire and light attacks.

Instead of completely negating the effect, successful resistance reduces damage to **half**.

If a saving throw also reduces the damage by half, the scion takes **one-quarter damage**.

### Brenna

Resistance applies to magical effects that slow, hold, entangle, immobilize, or otherwise restrain movement.

### Masela

Resistance applies to drowning and hostile water-based effects.

If the scion would drown, make the resistance roll.

If successful, the scion survives and thereafter gains the ability to **breathe water permanently**.

### Reynir

Resistance applies to magical cold, mundane cold, hunger, exposure, and severe natural weather.

Damage is reduced to **half** on a successful resistance roll.

If a saving throw also reduces damage by half, the scion suffers **one-quarter damage**.

### Vorynn

Resistance applies to arcane spells classified as **Invocation/Evocation**.

For damaging effects, successful resistance reduces damage to **half**.

If a successful saving throw also reduces the damage by half, the scion suffers **one-quarter damage**.

## Shadow Form

**Strength:** Great  
**Derivations:** Brenna, Azrai

Once per day, the scion may transform himself and all carried equipment into living shadow.

The transformation lasts:

**1 turn + 1 round per character level**

While in Shadow Form:

- The scion is two-dimensional.
- He is effectively invisible when motionless in natural shadow or darkness.
- He may move across walls, ceilings, water, and hazardous surfaces as easily as ordinary ground.
- Mundane physical barriers do not impede his movement if a shadow could naturally cross them.
- He can be harmed only by magical weapons, spells, and other magical effects.
- Undead normally ignore him unless given a reason to recognize him as living.

The scion cannot:

- Attack.
- Cast spells.
- Manipulate objects.
- Open doors.
- Pick locks.
- Carry additional objects.
- Fly.

## Touch of Decay

**Strength:** Great  
**Derivation:** Azrai

Once per day, the scion may destroy inanimate matter by touch.

The ability affects up to:

**10 cubic feet + 1 cubic foot per character level**

Metal rusts away, wood rots, leather crumbles, and stone breaks down into sand and debris.

Unattended mundane objects are normally destroyed automatically.

A magical object receives a saving throw determined by the Referee.

If an object is being worn or carried by another creature, the scion must first make a successful attack roll. The bearer may then make a saving throw to prevent the object's destruction.

Approximately once per month, the power may manifest accidentally at the Referee's discretion.

## Travel

**Strength:** Great  
**Derivations:** All except Anduiras and Reynir

The scion possesses a limited supernatural form of long-distance travel.

The scion may carry all worn and carried equipment and may bring **one willing companion per character level**.

The ability may be used once per week, plus one additional time per week for every full five character levels.

| Level | Uses per Week |
|---|---:|
| 1–4 | 1 |
| 5–9 | 2 |
| 10–14 | 3 |
| 15–19 | 4 |

The scion must either have previously visited the destination or studied it through magical scrying.

### Azrai

Between sunset and sunrise, the scion can enter the Shadow World from a place where the boundary is thin and travel through it in the manner of a halfling.

### Basaiä

The scion may enter a fire large enough to completely surround his body and emerge from another fire of similar size at the destination.

The scion is protected from the flames during transit.

### Brenna

While standing upon a road, trail, or path, the scion may travel instantly to another point along the same connected network of roads and paths.

### Masela

While standing in the shallows of a body of water, the scion may travel instantly to any other point along the shore of that same body of water.

### Vorynn

At moonrise or moonset, the scion may travel instantly to a known destination.

The power functions only during the brief period surrounding moonrise or moonset.

## Unreadable Thoughts

**Strength:** Minor  
**Derivations:** All

The scion's thoughts cannot be read by ESP, telepathy, empathy, or similar magic.

The ability also blocks magical attempts to directly determine the scion's emotions, plans, or intentions.

If Character Reading is used against a character with Unreadable Thoughts, the two abilities cancel and provide no special information.

## Berserker's Blood

**Strength:** Great  
**Derivations:** Azrai, Vorynn

When the scion is reduced to **0 hit points or whatever threshold your campaign normally uses for incapacitation**, he may immediately make a saving throw.

The scion gains **+1 on this saving throw for every two character levels**.

If successful, the scion remains conscious and enters a berserk fury.

While berserk:

- The scion may use only melee weapons or unarmed attacks.
- He must attack the nearest enemy.
- When that enemy falls, he attacks the next nearest enemy.
- If no enemies remain, the scion must make a saving throw or attack the nearest living creature, including an ally.

The scion continues fighting until:

- He is healed above the campaign's incapacitation threshold.
- No living targets remain.
- Damage suffered beyond the threshold equals his **Bloodline Strength**.

If damage beyond the threshold equals his Bloodline Strength, the scion collapses into a coma.

If he is not restored above the incapacitation threshold within **2 rounds**, he dies.

If healed, the scion immediately falls into a deep sleep and must rest for **twice the number of rounds spent berserk**.

If he attempts strenuous activity before completing this rest, he must make a saving throw each round or immediately collapse and die.

After recovering, the scion makes one final saving throw.

Failure causes the permanent loss of **1 point of Bloodline Strength**.

This point may be regained normally through play, but cannot be restored by ordinary healing magic.

## Forest Walk

**Strength:** Minor, Major, Great  
**Derivation:** Reynir

### Minor

The scion moves through forest terrain at his normal movement rate regardless of undergrowth or ordinary natural obstacles.

He leaves no trail that can be followed by normal tracking methods, including scent.

### Major

The scion gains the Minor ability.

While in a forest, magical attempts to locate or track the scion have a **5% chance per character level** to fail.

### Great

The scion gains the Minor and Major abilities.

By entering a continuous forest, the scion may choose any point along that same forest as his destination.

Travel requires:

**24 hours - one-half the scion's character level**

During this period the scion travels normally, may encounter creatures, and may stop briefly to rest.

At the end of the required travel time, the scion instantaneously arrives at the chosen destination.

The destination must be chosen when the Forest Walk begins.

## Home Hearkening

**Strength:** Major, Great  
**Derivation:** Brenna

### Major

Whenever one of the regent's provinces or holdings faces a significant threat, contest, monster event, rebellion, or similar danger, the regent immediately senses that something is wrong.

The feeling persists until the problem is resolved.

There is no range limit.

### Great

The scion gains the Major ability.

The regent may also send a simple empathic impression of urgency and intent to a family member, close friend, vassal, or lieutenant within the threatened domain.

This communication is one-way and cannot convey detailed speech, but can communicate simple intentions such as:

- Defend this place.
- Seek me.
- Flee.
- Investigate.
- Delay the enemy.

A nonregent may instead develop Home Hearkening toward a permanent home after living there for at least six months.

## Light of Reason

**Strength:** Minor, Major, Great  
**Derivation:** Basaiä

### Minor

Up to **3 times per day**, the scion may radiate light as the **Light** spell.

The light lasts **1 turn per character level**.

### Major

The scion gains the Minor ability.

Once per day, the scion may emit a brilliant flash in a **30-foot radius**.

Creatures that can see the flash must make a saving throw or be blinded for:

**1 round per 2 character levels, rounded up**

A creature that knows the flash is coming may avert its eyes and avoid the effect.

Scions possessing Light of Reason are immune to this flash.

### Great

The scion gains the Minor and Major abilities.

## Mebhaighl Sense

**Strength:** Minor, Major, Great  
**Derivation:** Vorynn

### Minor

The scion automatically senses whenever an arcane spell is cast within **100 yards**.

He knows the general direction of the spellcasting but nothing more.

### Major

The scion gains the Minor ability.

He also knows the **school of arcane magic** involved.

If the scion is unfamiliar with formal schools of magic, the Referee instead describes the general character of the magic.

### Great

The scion gains the Minor and Major abilities.

By concentrating upon an area up to **100 yards across**, the scion can determine whether arcane magic has been cast there recently.

He can sense magical activity dating back:

**1 week per character level**

He also learns the school of magic involved.

Realm magic can be detected in the same fashion.

Mebhaighl Sense does not detect Cleric or Druid spells.

## Sea Song

**Strength:** Major  
**Derivation:** Masela

The scion can listen to the supernatural memory of a large body of water.

The scion must stand beside or within a:

- Sea.
- Ocean.
- Bay.
- Large lake.
- Major river.

The scion may invoke Sea Song **once per week for every three character levels**.

Each use allows the scion to ask **one question per character level**.

Questions must concern:

- The body of water itself.
- Creatures or vessels traveling upon or within it.
- Weather affecting it.
- Lands directly touching its shores.

Questions should normally permit simple answers.

The water answers truthfully to the best of its supernatural awareness but may be vague or incomplete.

Sea Song concerns only the specific body of water contacted and does not automatically extend into adjoining rivers, bays, seas, or tributaries.
## Regency
Blooded rulers possess the supernatural ability to gather and wield Regency Points (RP).
Regency represents divine authority, loyalty, legitimacy, reputation, political influence, and mystical connection to the land and its people.
A regent collects RP from:
- Provinces
- Law holdings
- Temple holdings
- Guild holdings
- Source holdings
- Bloodline Strength
- The loyalty of subjects and followers
#### Why Regency Matters
Regency allows rulers to strengthen domain actions, resist rivals, empower decrees, support armies, cast realm magic, and maintain authority over their domains.
## Who can Rule?
Technically, anyone may attempt to rule a domain, In practice, blooded rulers possess enormous advantages because they can gather and spend Regency Points.
A powerful unblooded ruler may command armies and wealth, but blooded dynasties carry divine legitimacy recognized throughout Cerilia.
## Types of Regents

| Regent Type   | Typical Holdings                            |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Noble Regent  | Provinces and law holdings                  |
| Temple Regent | Temple holdings and religious authority     |
| Guild Regent  | Guild holdings, trade routes, and commerce  |
| Source Regent | Magical sources, ley lines, and realm magic |
## Realm Magic
The greatest blooded wizards and priests can cast realm spells - powerful magic affecting entire provinces, armies, weather patterns, harvests, and kingdoms.
Realm magic is far beyond ordinary adventuring spells and requires:
- Bloodline power
- Regency
- Holdings
- Domain influence
- Time and preparation
A true realm spell may decide the fate of nations.
## Bloodtheft
When a blooded scion dies violently, fragments of divine power may pass to the killer in an event known as bloodtheft.
Bloodtheft is feared throughout Cerilia because it allows ambitious rulers, monsters, and tyrants to strengthen their own bloodlines by slaying rivals.
Some awnsheghlien have grown monstrous over centuries through repeated acts of bloodtheft.
## The Awnsheghlien
Some bloodlines, especially those descending from Azrai, slowly corrupt their possessors. Over time, these scions become transformed physically, mentally, spiritually, or magically into monstrous beings known as awnsheghlien.
Many awnsheghlien rule entire realms through terror and supernatural power.
### Bloodlines and Adventure
Bloodlines are more than political tools. They shape personal destiny, family legacy, supernatural power, and the future of entire realms.
A blooded adventurer may:
- Claim ancient heirlooms
- Inherit provinces or holdings
- Become the target of rivals
- Awaken new blood abilities
- Found a new dynasty
- Challenge existing rulers
- Change the fate of nations
### Not Every Hero Is Blooded

# Lockpicking

Locks and lockpicks are rated from **Type I through Type VI**. Higher-quality lockpicks are more effective against difficult locks.

A character must possess a set of lockpicks to attempt to pick a lock.

## Lockpicking Training

### Thieves

Thieves are trained in lockpicking.

When a Thief uses a lockpick, treat the lockpick as **one Type higher** than its actual Type for purposes of opening the lock.

A Type VI lockpick cannot be improved beyond Type VI.

**Examples:**

- Type I → treated as Type II
- Type II → treated as Type III
- Type III → treated as Type IV
- Type V → treated as Type VI
- Type VI → remains Type VI

This bonus represents the Thief's training and replaces the normal Thief lock-opening percentage when using this lockpicking system.

### Non-Thieves

Characters who are not Thieves may attempt to pick locks, but treat the lockpick as **one Type lower** than its actual Type.

**Examples:**

- Type VI → treated as Type V
- Type IV → treated as Type III
- Type II → treated as Type I

A Type I lockpick cannot be reduced below Type I. Instead, an untrained character using a Type I lockpick works at **half the normal attempt rate**:

- **1 attempt every 2 melee rounds**, or
- **5 attempts during a 10-minute dungeon turn**

This represents the character struggling with the simplest available tools without proper training.
## Effective Lockpick Type

Determine the lockpick's **Effective Type** before making any rolls.

Apply modifiers in this order:

1. Start with the lockpick's actual Type.
2. Thief: **+1 Type**
3. Non-Thief: **-1 Type**
4. Duress: **-1 additional Type**
5. Minimum Effective Type is **Type I**.
6. Maximum Effective Type is **Type VI**.

If a Non-Thief would be reduced below Type I, the lockpick remains Type I but the character makes attempts at half the normal rate.

### Example

A Thief using a Type III lockpick normally treats it as **Type IV**.

If the Thief is under duress, the -1 Type penalty cancels the Thief's +1 Type bonus, so the lockpick functions as **Type III**.

A non-Thief using a Type III lockpick treats it as **Type II**. If that character is also under duress, it functions as **Type I**.
## Time Required

Under normal circumstances, a character may make:

- **1 lockpicking attempt per melee round**, or
- **10 lockpicking attempts during one 10-minute dungeon turn**

An untrained character using a Type I lockpick may make only:

- **1 attempt every 2 melee rounds**, or
- **5 attempts per dungeon turn**

Each roll represents continued work on the same lock.

Successful results that must be accumulated, such as "two 6s," remain accumulated between attempts as long as the character continues working on the lock.
## Duress

A character attempting to pick a lock while under significant pressure treats the lockpick as **one Type lower**.

Examples of duress include:

- Active combat occurring nearby.
- Being directly threatened.
- Extreme weather.
- Severe fatigue.
- Working while injured or in an unstable position.
- Other distracting circumstances determined by the Referee.

The Referee determines when duress applies.
## Lockpicking Table

Cross-reference the character's **Effective Lockpick Type** with the **Lock Type**.

| Effective Lockpick | Lock I | Lock II | Lock III | Lock IV | Lock V | Lock VI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **two 6s** required | 1d6; **three 6s** required | 1d6; **four 6s** required | 1d6; **five 6s** required | 1d6; **six 6s** required |
| **II** | 1d6; **5–6** unlocks | 1d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **two 6s** required | 1d6; **three 6s** required | 1d6; **four 6s** required | 1d6; **five 6s** required |
| **III** | 1d6; **4–6** unlocks | 1d6; **5–6** unlocks | 1d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **two 6s** required | 1d6; **three 6s** required | 1d6; **four 6s** required |
| **IV** | 2d6; **5–6** unlocks | 2d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **5–6** unlocks | 1d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **two 6s** required | 1d6; **three 6s** required |
| **V** | 2d6; **4–6** unlocks | 2d6; **5–6** unlocks | 2d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **5–6** unlocks | 1d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **two 6s** required |
| **VI** | 4d6; **4–6** unlocks | 2d6; **4–6** unlocks | 2d6; **5–6** unlocks | 2d6; **6** unlocks | 1d6; **5–6** unlocks | 1d6; **6** unlocks |

### Reading the Table

When the result lists a range such as:

> **2d6; 5–6 unlocks**

roll all listed dice. If **any die** produces the indicated result, the lock opens.

When the result instead requires multiple 6s, such as:

> **1d6; three 6s required**

each 6 counts as one success. Successes accumulate between attempts until the required number has been rolled.

### Example

A character with an Effective Type III lockpick attempts a Type IV lock.

The table reads:

> **1d6; two 6s required**

The character rolls once each melee round.

If they roll:

- Round 1: **3**
- Round 2: **6**
- Round 3: **2**
- Round 4: **6**

the lock opens at the end of Round 4.
## Lockpick Damage

Lockpicking can damage the tools being used.

If **any die shows a natural 1** during a lockpicking attempt, the lockpick is permanently reduced by **one actual Type** after resolving that attempt.

Only one Type is lost per attempt, even if multiple dice show 1s.

Examples:

- Type IV → Type III
- Type III → Type II
- Type II → Type I

If a **Type I** lockpick is reduced, it breaks and becomes unusable.

The character must obtain or purchase replacement tools.

> [!important]
> Damage reduces the lockpick's **actual Type**, not merely its Effective Type.
>
> A Thief whose Type III lockpick is damaged now owns a Type II lockpick, which the Thief treats as Effective Type III because of their class bonus.

# Domain Play

Regency, rulership, provinces, holdings, taxes, armies, diplomacy, and the seasonal turn of power

Birthright adds a second layer to Swords & Wizardry play. Characters may still explore ruins, fight monsters, and seek treasure, but blooded scions may also rule provinces, command temples, control guilds, forge ley lines, raise armies, and shape the fate of Cerilia.

## What Is Domain Play?

Domain play is the large-scale game of rulership. It tracks what happens when characters control lands, holdings, armies, trade routes, churches, guilds, and magical sources.

A domain turn represents approximately three months. During that time, regents collect income, gain Regency Points, pay maintenance, respond to random events, take actions, wage wars, and adjust the loyalty of their people.

### Adventuring Still Matters

Domain play does not replace normal Swords & Wizardry adventuring. Instead, it gives adventures larger consequences. A dungeon expedition might recover a royal charter, expose a rival regent, secure a lost temple relic, or prevent an enemy from contesting your holdings.

## Regents and Domains

A regent is a character who rules at least one province or holding. Most regents are blooded scions, because only blooded characters can fully collect and use Regency Points.

A domain may be a kingdom, barony, temple network, merchant guild, source network, military order, or any combination of these.

| Regent Type   | Typical Domain                  | Common S&W Classes                            |
| ------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Realm Regent  | Provinces and law holdings      | Fighter, Paladin, Ranger, Chivalric Knight    |
| Temple Regent | Temple holdings                 | Cleric, Druid, Dwarven Priest                 |
| Guild Regent  | Guild holdings and trade routes | Thief, Assassin, Troubadour                   |
| Source Regent | Sources and ley lines           | Magic-User, Illusionist, Necromancer, Warlock |

## Provinces

A province is a settled region of land. Its level measures population, development, towns, roads, farms, and general civilization.

| Province Level | Player-Facing Description                                                              |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0              | Wilderness, ruins, monster-haunted lands, or almost uninhabited territory.             |
| 1–3            | Thinly settled rural lands, small villages, frontier farms, and a few local towns.     |
| 4–6            | Settled farmlands, market towns, local industries, and stronger administration.        |
| 7+             | Densely settled provinces with major cities, strong trade, and developed institutions. |

Each province also has a source potential, written after the slash in domain records. A province written as Alamier (4/3) is a province level 4 with magical source potential 3.

Civilization weakens wild magic. As province level rises, source potential generally falls.

## Holdings

Holdings represent organized influence inside a province. A ruler may control the land, but other regents may control the law, faith, trade, or magic within that same province.

| Holding | What It Represents                                                                                                       |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Law     | Courts, sheriffs, guards, royal officials, soldiers, patrols, bureaucracy, taxation enforcement, or even bandit control. |
| Temple  | Churches, shrines, priesthoods, monasteries, sacred authority, doctrine, and public faith.                               |
| Guild   | Merchants, craftsmen, trade networks, caravans, smugglers, docks, warehouses, and criminal organizations.                |
| Source  | The natural flow of mebhaighl, magical places, sacred groves, ancient stones, wildlands, and arcane power.               |

Holding Slots

Law, temple, and guild holdings usually share slots equal to the province level. A province (4) can support up to 4 levels of law, 4 levels of temple, and 4 levels of guild holdings. Source holdings use source potential instead.

| Province Level | Number of Regents Who May Divide One Holding Type      |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1–3            | One regent controls that holding type.                 |
| 4–6            | One or two regents may divide that holding type.       |
| 7–9            | As many as three regents may divide that holding type. |

Level 0 holdings do not count against this limit.

## Regency Points

Regency Points, or RP, represent divine authority, reputation, legitimacy, political momentum, loyalty, and the supernatural bond between a blooded ruler and a domain.

Regents collect RP from appropriate provinces and holdings. A regent’s class and domain type determine what sources of RP they can fully claim.

### Using Regency

RP may be spent to support your own domain actions, oppose another regent’s actions, influence political outcomes, empower realm spells, and protect your interests.

## Gold Bars

Gold Bars, or GB, represent large-scale wealth. One GB is approximately 2,000 gold pieces.

GB are used for taxes, armies, construction, courts, diplomacy, fortifications, ships, trade routes, and other domain-scale costs.

## The Domain Turn Sequence

A domain turn represents roughly three months. Each domain turn contains three action rounds, each of which represents about one month.

| Step | Domain Turn Procedure                                                                                                                                         |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Referee Rolls Random Events. Determine unusual crises, opportunities, threats, or adventure hooks affecting active domains.                                   |
| 2    | Determine Domain Initiative. Each regent rolls 1d10 and adds character level or Hit Dice. This order lasts for the entire domain turn.                        |
| 3    | Collect Regency Points. Regents total RP from their provinces, holdings, vassals, and other valid sources.                                                    |
| 4    | Taxation, Collection, and Trade. Province rulers tax provinces. Temple and guild regents collect income. Trade routes produce GB.                             |
| 5    | Pay Maintenance Costs. Pay for armies, ships, courts, fortifications, ley lines, and other ongoing expenses.                                                  |
| 6    | Declare Free Actions. Resolve decrees, routine administration, disbanding, movement inside friendly areas, and other free actions.                            |
| 7    | First Action Round.  <br>A. Domain Actions  <br>B. War Moves, if applicable  <br>C. Fight Battles, if applicable  <br>D. Occupation or Retreat, if applicable |
| 8    | Second Action Round. Repeat the same A-D sequence.                                                                                                            |
| 9    | Third Action Round. Repeat the same A-D sequence.                                                                                                             |
| 10   | Adjust Loyalty and Regency. Apply loyalty changes, ignored event penalties, occupation effects, rebellion effects, taxation consequences, and regency losses. |

### Action Rounds and Adventures

A character adventure may consume an action round if it takes about one month of campaign time. This allows classic Swords & Wizardry adventures to remain part of domain play.

If war occurs during an action round, complete domain actions first, then resolve War Moves, battles, and occupation or retreat.

## Taxation, Collection, and Trade

During Step 4, regents collect money. Province rulers tax their provinces, temple and guild regents collect income from holdings, and trade routes produce revenue.

### Province Taxation

Province rulers choose light, moderate, or severe taxation for each province.

| Province Rating | Light Taxes | Moderate Taxes | Severe Taxes |
| --------------- | ----------- | -------------- | ------------ |
| 0               | —           | —              | d3-2*        |
| 1               | d3-2*       | d3-1*          | d3           |
| 2               | d3-1*       | d3             | d4           |
| 3               | d3          | d4             | d4+1         |
| 4               | d4          | d4+1           | d6+1         |
| 5               | d4+1        | d6+1           | d8+1         |
| 6               | d6+1        | d8+1           | d10+1        |
| 7               | d8+1        | d10+1          | d12+1        |
| 8               | d10+1       | d12+1          | 2d8          |

| Province Rating | Holding 1 | Holding 2 | Holding 3 | Holding 4–5 | Holding 6+ |

### Claims by Law Holdings

Law holdings may claim part of the money generated by province taxation or by guild and temple collection in the same province. This represents sheriffs, tax collectors, tolls, customs duties, legal fees, seizure, protection payments, or simple pressure.

The Referee compares the law holding to the target province or holding and determines whether the law holder claims any GB from that revenue.

### Trade Routes

Trade routes generate GB during the taxation and collection step. A land trade route normally requires a road and connects provinces of different terrain or culture.

Sea trade routes may be established from coastal or river provinces of level 4 or higher. A sea route to another province is worth the average of the two provinces, rounded up. A route to a sea lane or distant “parts unknown” is worth half the port province’s rating.

### Ships and Sea Trade

Sea trade requires ships with enough cargo capacity to carry the route’s GB value. If the ships cannot carry all the cargo, the extra GB are lost until more or larger ships are assigned.

See Custom Domain Rules: Sea Trade Routes for coastal-route exceptions and ship-capacity details. 

## Maintenance Costs

During Step 5, regents pay upkeep. A powerful domain may collapse quickly if the treasury cannot support its obligations.

- Armies require regular maintenance.
- Ships require maintenance.
- Courts and administration require funding.
- Castles, fortifications, and roads may require support.
- Ley lines may require maintenance.

An unpaid army is not merely useless. It may become a danger to its own ruler.

## Resolving Domain Actions

Many domain actions require a success roll. Roll 1d20. If the result equals or exceeds the action’s success number, the action succeeds.

Regents may spend RP to improve their chances. Each RP spent lowers the success number by 1, but never below 1. Other affected regents may spend RP to oppose or support the action if they rule the province or hold influence there.

## Master Domain Action Reference

Birthright Domain Actions Reference

Use: This is a consolidated, campaign-table reference for domain actions from the Birthright core rules, major supplemental material, and approved campaign house rules. It is written in paraphrased, gameable language for import into a Foundry journal or item description.

Abbreviations: GB = Gold Bars; RP = Regency Points. “Domain” actions affect a regent’s domain. “Character” actions are personal undertakings. “Free” actions do not count against a regent’s three domain-turn actions. “Realm” means the action can be applied across multiple provinces or holdings when the rules allow.

## How Domain Actions Work

A domain turn represents three months and is divided into three action rounds. A regent normally receives three actions per domain turn, not counting free actions. Many actions require a d20 success roll; the roll succeeds if it equals or exceeds the listed success number. Regents may usually spend RP to improve or oppose the action, and affected regents may counterbid if they rule or hold power in the province involved.

Realm actions are broad versions of certain actions. They can target multiple provinces in a single realm or multiple holdings in a single province, but the regent must pay costs separately for each target and resolve each target separately when required.

# Core Action Notes

## Adventure

**Type:** Character  
**Cost:** None  
**Success:** Special

The regent personally undertakes an adventure.

A prolonged adventure lasting more than four weeks uses one action for each month the regent remains away.

While away, the regent cannot normally issue orders to the domain.

---

## Agitate

**Type:** Domain, Realm  
**Cost:** 1 RP, 1 GB  
**Success:** 10+

A regent with a holding may attempt to raise or lower the loyalty of a province.

A province ruler may agitate in favor of himself.

A priest regent may perform **one Agitate action per domain turn as a Free Action**.

The basic success number is modified by the difference between the agitator's holding and the province level.

The province ruler's Law holding can support or oppose the action.

If the action succeeds by **10 or more**, province loyalty changes by two grades rather than one.

---

## Build

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** 1 GB+; total varies by project  
**Success:** 5+

Build covers ordinary construction such as:

- Roads
- Bridges
- Halls
- Government buildings
- Warehouses
- Chapels
- Lighthouses
- Palaces

Build does **not** construct castles or fortified holdings.

Construction normally progresses at **1d6 GB per domain turn**.

Terrain affects construction costs:

- Province 2–3: 150% normal cost
- Province 0–1: 200% normal cost
- Extreme conditions may double the cost again

If the PC personally designs and supervises the work, Build counts as one of the character's normal actions rather than merely a Free Action.

---

## Contest

**Type:** Domain, Realm  
**Cost:** 1 RP  
**Success:** 10+

Contest neutralizes another regent's holding.

A contested holding:

- Generates no RP.
- Generates no GB.

A level 0 holding is destroyed immediately when successfully contested.

If an already-contested holding is successfully contested again, the owner loses it and its slots become uncontrolled.

A contested holding remains contested until:

- The attacker relents.
- The attacker loses all holdings or province control there.
- The defender successfully Rules the holding.

Province rulership may also be contested under certain circumstances.

---

## Create Holding

**Type:** Domain  
**Cost:** 1 GB  
**Success:** 10+

Creates a **level 0 holding** in a province where the regent currently has no holding of that type.

The province ruler and regents with similar holdings may support or oppose the attempt.

---

## Create Province

**Type:** Domain  
**Cost:** As Create Holding; tripled if not adjacent  
**Success:** 10+

This is presented under **Create Holding** in the Rulebook rather than as a completely separate entry.

A successful attempt creates a **level 0 province** in previously unclaimed land.

If the new province does not border a province already ruled by the regent, the cost is tripled.

---

## Declare War

**Type:** Domain  
**Cost:** None  
**Success:** Special

A regent must Declare War before moving military units into another regent's territory without permission.

After declaring war, the regent receives **four War Moves** following that action round.

A defender does not need to Declare War merely to defend his own territory.

---

## Decree

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** 1 GB  
**Success:** Special

Decree is a catch-all for official orders that do not warrant another domain action.

Examples include:

- Seizing assets.
- Imposing tariffs.
- Arresting or obstructing individuals.
- Passing laws.
- Creating a minor random event.
- Producing a moderate roleplaying effect.

A Decree cannot:

- Directly change a province or holding level.
- Directly change loyalty if another action is required.
- Affect another domain in a way that should require another domain action.

The Rulebook advises limiting a regent to roughly one or two significant decrees per domain turn.

---

## Diplomacy

**Type:** Domain  
**Cost:** 1 RP, 1 GB  
**Success:** 10+

Diplomacy can:

- Create or break alliances.
- Obtain permission for trade.
- Force concessions.
- Negotiate tribute or territory.
- Respond to diplomatic events.
- Reach other agreements permitted by the Referee.

The success number is modified by the difference between the acting regent's capital province or holding and the target's capital province or holding.

Court, palace, RP, GB, and situational modifiers may also apply.

Against a PC regent, the player ultimately decides whether the proposed agreement is accepted.

---

## Disband

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** None  
**Success:** Automatic; 5+ for mercenaries

Regular army units may be disbanded automatically.

Mercenaries require a **5+ success roll**.

If the roll fails, the mercenaries may become brigands.

Holdings and castles may also be voluntarily abandoned.

---

## Espionage

**Type:** Domain; Thief Free; Realm for military intelligence  
**Cost:** 1 GB  
**Success:** Special

The base success number is **20**.

The number is improved by:

- Target province level.
- The acting regent's guild holdings in the province.

Loyal Law holdings defending the province worsen attempts aimed at the ruler.

Espionage may:

- Discover diplomatic agreements.
- Reveal troop movements and military strength.
- Conduct assassinations.
- Create intrigue, corruption, or heresy.
- Identify another spy.
- Rescue prisoners.
- Secretly move people or messages.

If the action fails by **10 or more**, the target learns the spy's identity.

A Realm Espionage action may affect several provinces, but only troop strength and movement information may be gathered that way.

---

## Finances

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** None  
**Success:** Automatic

Conversion rate:

**1 GB = 2,000 gp**

A regent may normally convert up to:

**5 GB + 1 GB per guild holding level controlled**

per domain turn.

Finances may be used only once per domain turn.

---

## Forge Ley Line

**Type:** Domain  
**Cost:** 1 RP and 1 GB per province crossed  
**Success:** 5+

A wizard creates a magical conduit connecting source holdings.

The line allows a weaker source to draw upon the strength of a connected stronger source for realm spellcasting.

A ley line costs **1 RP per domain turn to maintain**, regardless of length.

---

## Fortify

**Type:** Domain, Realm  
**Base Cost:** 1 RP  
**Success:** 2+

### Fortified Holding

Cost:

**4 GB per holding level**

Normal construction:

**1d6 GB progress per domain turn**

Accelerated construction:

Pay **5 GB per level** to progress at **2d6 GB per domain turn**.

### Castle

Cost:

**8 GB per castle level**

Normal construction:

**1d6 GB progress per domain turn**

Accelerated construction:

Pay **10 GB per turn** to progress at **2d6 GB per domain turn**.

A castle may exceed the province level, but construction costs double for levels above the province level.

A castle automatically fortifies the ruler's holdings in that province up to the castle's level.

## Grant

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** Variable  
**Success:** 10+

Grant may be used to:

- Give money.
- Bestow titles.
- Sponsor artists or scholars.
- Fund expeditions.
- Elevate commoners.
- Increase the loyalty of a nonrebellious province by one grade.
- Address certain random events.

The success number worsens by 1 for each GB spent and for each noble title bestowed.

## Hold Action

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** None  
**Success:** Automatic

The regent delays an action until the end of the action round.

The held action must respond to a development or provocation.

If nothing occurs and the action is not used, it is lost.

---

## Investiture

**Type:** Domain; Realm in applicable cases  
**Cost:** Special  
**Success:** Special

Investiture requires a priest capable of casting the **Investiture realm spell**.

The priest performs the ceremony as a Free Action, but at least one regent involved must expend a Domain Action.

Investiture may transfer:

- Provinces.
- Holdings.
- Entire domains.
- Vassalage.
- Bloodlines.

### Province or Holding

Base success:

**10+**

Cost:

**RP equal to the province or holding level**

The target must normally have been conquered or contested if the transfer is unwilling.

### Vassalage

The liege and vassal agree upon an amount of RP paid each domain turn.

### Bloodline Investiture

Transfers an entire bloodline from donor to recipient.

### Entire Domain

The recipient pays RP equal to the **Domain Power** of the domain being transferred.

A willing transfer succeeds automatically.

An unwilling transfer uses a success number of 10 modified by the difference in bloodline strength.

## Lieutenant

**Type:** Character  
**Cost:** None  
**Success:** Special

The regent recruits a lieutenant.

Once per domain turn, one lieutenant may perform a Domain Action for the regent.

The regent still pays all normal costs.

No additional RP may be spent to improve the lieutenant's success chance.

## Move Troops

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** 1 GB per 10 unit/province moves  
**Success:** Automatic by land

Examples:

- 1 unit across 10 provinces = 1 GB
- 10 units across 1 province = 1 GB
- 5 units across 2 provinces = 1 GB

Troops moving by land require one full action round to relocate.

The regent must Declare War or receive permission to move through foreign territory.

### Core Sea Movement

The original Rulebook requires **1 GB per unit** and a success roll reflecting ship availability and weather.

The later naval rules replace this with the separate **Move Troops by Sea** action.

**Type:** Free  
**Cost:** Varies by unit  
**Success:** Automatic

A province may muster a number of units up to its province level in one domain turn.

Newly mustered regular units are not available until the next action round.

Mercenaries are available immediately.

Mustering militia temporarily reduces the province level by 1.

**Type:** Character  
**Cost:** None  
**Success:** Special

A character uses a profession, secondary skill, craft, performance, or similar activity to earn personal wealth.

Profits depend upon the occupation and province level.

**Cost:** Spell-specific  
**Success:** Special

A priest or wizard regent casts a realm spell.

Priests require an appropriate Temple holding.

Wizards require a Source holding or suitable Ley Line.

## Research

**Type:** Character  
**Cost:** 1 GB+  
**Success:** Special

A wizard or priest may:

- Research new spells.
- Create magical items.
- Conduct other magical research permitted by the rules.

## Rule

**Type:** Domain, Realm  
**Cost:** Variable  
**Success:** 10+

Raises a province or holding by one level.

### Holding

Cost:

**1 GB + RP equal to the target level**

Example:

Guild 3 → Guild 4 costs:

**1 GB + 4 RP**

### Province

Cost:

**1 GB and 1 RP per target level**

Example:

Province 3 → Province 4 costs:

**4 GB + 4 RP**

Ruling from level 0 to level 1 costs **1 GB and no RP**.

Multiple holdings may be Ruled with a Realm Action.

## Trade Route

**Type:** Domain, Realm  
**Cost:** 1 RP, 1 GB  
**Success:** 10+

Requires a guild holding.

An overland trade route must generally:

- Connect provinces of different terrain.
- Have a road connecting the major settlements.

A standard route generates GB equal to the average of the two province levels.

A route to distant "parts unknown" generates half the port province's level.

The number of routes a province can support is limited by province level.

## Exploratory Trade

**Type:** Domain or Character  
**Cost:** 1 RP, 5 GB + cargo investment  
**Success:** 20+

Requires a guild holding in a seaport province of level 4+.

A regent may attempt this from a particular province only once every four domain turns.

Modifiers include:

- Guilder: +1
- Regent personally accompanies voyage: +2
- Guilder lieutenant accompanies instead: +1

Natural 1:

- Disaster.
- Ships, cargo, and possibly lieutenants may be lost.

Failure:

- Cargo investment returns after two months.
- Voyage costs and RP/GB spent on the roll are lost.

Success:

**Profit = Port Province Level × GB invested in cargo**

The voyage takes two months.

## Progress

**Type:** Realm  
**Cost:** 1 RP, 1 GB  
**Success:** Special; normally 50+

Used to introduce major:

- Social advances.
- Technological advances.
- Magical advances.
- Economic advances.

The default success number should generally be **50 or higher**, unless the proposed advance is a relatively modest extension of existing knowledge.

## Sea Trade Route

**Type:** Domain, Realm  
**Cost:** 1 RP, 1 GB  
**Success:** 10+

This expands the standard Trade Route rules.

A port is any coastal or river province of level 4+.

The other end must normally:

- Be within one month's sailing distance.
- Be a coastal province.
- Have different terrain or belong to a different culture.

A ship may travel approximately **30 maritime areas per month**.

Route value:

**Average of the two province levels, rounded up**

A route to distant "parts unknown" yields half the originating port's level.

## Trade Chain

**Type:** Domain  
**Cost:** 2 GB, 2 RP per added link  
**Success:** 10+

Extends an existing trade route from its terminus.

Trade chain value:

1. Average all province levels in the chain.
2. Round up.
3. Add **1 GB for each province beyond the second**.

Trade chains allow long-distance commerce beyond the normal single-route sailing limit.

If an interior link collapses, all links beyond it also collapse.

## Trade Service

**Type:** Domain, Realm  
**Cost:** 1 RP per holding  
**Success:** 10+

Two different regents with holdings in the same province may cooperate to create temporary economic benefits.

Any holding types may cooperate.

The resulting GB is:

**Average of the two holding levels, rounded up**

If the province ruler participates, average the province level and holding level instead.

Restrictions:

- A regent cannot use Trade Service with his own holdings.
- A regent cannot use it with a vassal's holdings.
- A holding or province level may participate only once per domain turn.
- Both parties must agree.

The generated GB is divided between the participants however they agree.
## Investiture Subtypes and Ceremonies

### Coronation

Investiture: A ceremony that confirms a blooded heir or claimant as the rightful regent of a domain. It may follow inheritance, conquest, recognition, or designation. Use this when the new ruler must be mystically bound to the land and domain.

### Designation

Investiture: A living regent names an heir or divides future inheritance among several heirs. This can prepare for a smooth succession, but it does not absolutely prevent crises, bloodtheft complications, or the land choosing differently when the ruler dies.

### Recognition

Investiture: One regent willingly transfers a province, holding, or recognized asset to another blooded character. It can create vassal regents, settle wars, formalize bargains, or legalize control over formerly uncontrolled assets.

### Divestiture

Investiture: A hostile or coercive rite stripping a regent’s bond to land, holdings, or domain assets. It is usually a dark or aggressive act unless directed against a monstrous tyrant or illegitimate ruler.

### Bloodline Investiture

Investiture: Transfer or bestow a bloodline from one character to another under ritual conditions. This can elevate an unblooded heir, complete a succession plan, or redirect divine inheritance.

### Vassalage

Investiture: Establishes a formal bond between liege and vassal, allowing regency or duty to flow according to the oath. Use this to model feudal, temple, guild, or magical subordination.

## Wizard Regent Notes

Wizard regents often gain the most from Create Holding, Forge Ley Line, Ley Link, Realm Spell, Research, Diplomacy, and Espionage. They usually avoid unnecessary Build and Rule Province actions because increasing settlement and roads can reduce source potential. A wizard’s long-term strength comes from protected sources, useful ley lines, allies who can provide GB or RP, and enough intelligence to avoid source wars that drain both sides.

## Guild and Trade Regent Notes

Guild regents benefit heavily from Trade Route, Sea Trade Route, Trade Chain, Exploratory Trade, Diplomacy, Espionage, and Decree negotiation with province rulers. A realm ruler may demand port fees, ship permission, or a cut of trade-route revenue, so successful guildmasters often treat diplomacy as part of the cost of business.

## Priest Regent Notes

Priest regents are strong with Agitate, Investiture, Diplomacy, Realm Spell, Decree, and Rule Temple. Their ability to agitate as a free action gives them significant influence over public loyalty, succession, legitimacy, and the moral standing of rulers.

## Military Regent Notes

Realm rulers, law regents, and warrior-kings rely on Muster Armies, Move Troops, Declare War, Fortify, Improve Unit, Decree, Diplomacy, and Investiture. A strong military domain is expensive, so Finances, taxation policy, and vassal arrangements matter as much as battlefield victories.

## Free Actions

Free actions do not consume one of the regent’s three normal domain actions. They usually represent routine orders, administration, or responses that do not require the full attention of the regent.

- Decree
- Disband
- Finances
- Grant
- Hold Action
- Move Troops within friendly limits
- Some priestly or magical support actions, when allowed by the rules
## Loyalty

Loyalty measures how much a province supports its ruler. It is rated High, Average, Poor, or Rebellious.

| Loyalty    | Effect                                                                                        |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| High       | The province strongly supports the ruler.                                                     |
| Average    | The province is stable.                                                                       |
| Poor       | Actions in the province cost +1 GB and +1 RP.                                                 |
| Rebellious | The province generates no taxes, adds nothing to Domain Power, and action costs double there. |

### End-of-Turn Loyalty Adjustments

| Cause                                                                | Loyalty Change                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Severe taxes were collected                                          | -1 grade                                         |
| No taxes were collected                                              | +1 grade                                         |
| Levies were mustered and sent to a foreign war started by the regent | -1 grade                                         |
| The regent ignored a random event affecting the province             | -1 grade                                         |
| A rival regent successfully agitated against the ruler               | -1 grade                                         |
| The ruler successfully agitated in support of the domain             | +1 grade                                         |
| The regent wins a major battle against a traditional enemy           | +1 grade to all provinces, at Referee judgment   |
| The province is under occupation                                     | -1 grade; newly occupied provinces begin at Poor |

### Law Holdings and Loyalty

Law holdings can control or suppress loyalty changes.

| Law Control                                                        | Effect on Loyalty Changes                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Regent controls all available law holdings                         | May ignore two grades of loyalty change.                                                                                                  |
| Regent controls at least half, but not all, available law holdings | May ignore one grade of loyalty change.                                                                                                   |
| Regent controls less than half available law holdings              | May ignore no loyalty changes. Severe taxation causes two grades of loyalty loss, and moderate taxation causes one grade of loyalty loss. |

## Rebellion

When a province becomes Rebellious, the ruler cannot collect taxes there, and the ruler’s holdings in that province are treated as contested.

In the next action round, the province may raise the largest levy possible and declare war against the occupying or offending forces. Raising this levy permanently reduces the province level by one until restored by later domain play.

## War Moves and Army Movement

During an action round involving war, War Moves occur after normal domain actions. Armies move by province, and terrain affects how quickly troops can move.

| Terrain          | Maximum Province Level | Movement Cost |
| ---------------- | ---------------------- | ------------- |
| Desert           | 3                      | 1             |
| Glacier          | 1                      | 4             |
| Heavy Forest     | 6                      | 2             |
| Hills            | 9                      | 2             |
| Light Forest     | 8                      | 1             |
| Marsh/Swamp      | 6                      | 2             |
| Low Mountains    | 7                      | 2             |
| Medium Mountains | 5                      | 3             |
| High Mountains   | 3                      | 4             |
| Moor/Highland    | 6                      | 2             |
| Plains           | 10                     | 1             |
| Steppes          | 6                      | 1             |
| Tundra           | 2                      | 2             |

A unit with a movement rating of 2 can normally cross two provinces in a War Move, but in most difficult terrain it is limited to one province per War Move.

## Military Units and Maintenance

Regents may muster army units such as infantry, archers, cavalry, knights, irregulars, scouts, mercenaries, and siege troops.

Armies require maintenance each domain turn. Unpaid troops may disband, mutiny, desert, raid the countryside, or become politically dangerous.

## Ships, Sea Movement, and Sea Trade

Ships add naval movement, sea trade, troop transport, and naval warfare to domain play.

| Ship Troop Capacity | Maintenance Cost per Domain Turn |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 0–3 units           | 1 GB                             |
| 4–6 units           | 2 GB                             |
| 7–9 units           | 3 GB                             |
| 10–12 units         | 4 GB                             |

A ship that is not maintained cannot move, fight, or perform useful tasks that turn. Continued neglect may permanently reduce its seaworthiness.

### Move Ships

Move Ships is a free action. A ship may move to support sea trade, carry troops, transport characters, or perform missions. Once ships are committed to normal movement in an action round, they are generally unavailable for War Moves later that round unless a penalty is paid to recall them.

### Move Troops by Sea

Moving troops by sea is a free action with a base cost of 1 GB per five units. Mounted troops count as two units. Troops must embark at a port and may disembark at coastal provinces. Landing troops in enemy territory requires Declare War.

## Realm Magic

Priest and wizard regents may cast realm spells as domain-scale magic. Realm spells can affect provinces, armies, weather, loyalty, sources, and more.

Wizard realm spells require sources or ley lines. Priest realm spells require temple holdings. Realm spells usually require RP, GB, and an action round of preparation or casting.
## Experience

## Adventure XP (AXP)

Player characters gain Adventure XP from:

- Defeating or overcoming monsters.
- Surviving dangerous challenges.
- Recovering treasure.

Treasure grants XP only when it is successfully returned to a safe settlement, stronghold, camp, or other secure haven.

**Treasure XP:** 1 gp = 1 XP

## Followers and Adventure XP

Adventure XP is first divided into shares based on the characters and followers who actually participated in the adventure.

|**Follower Type**|**Typical Starting Level**|**XP Share**|**Advancement**|**Primary Loyalty**|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Hireling|Level 0|None|Does not normally advance|Wages|
|Retainer|Level 0|None|Does not normally advance|Personal service|
|Henchman|Level 1+|1/2 share|Advances normally using XP actually earned|Personal loyalty|
|Bodyguard|Level 0+|1/4 share if actively participating|Advances normally using XP actually earned|Regent, domain, or household|

### XP Share Procedure

Count each participating PC as **1 full share**, each participating henchman as **1/2 share**, and each participating bodyguard as **1/4 share**.

Divide the total Adventure XP by the total number of shares, then award each participant XP according to the value of their share.

Hirelings and ordinary retainers do not normally receive XP.

### Example

Four PCs and one henchman recover **4,500 XP**.

Total shares:

- Four PCs = 4 shares.
- One henchman = 1/2 share.
- Total = 4.5 shares.

Each full share is worth **1,000 XP**.

Each PC receives **1,000 XP**, and the henchman receives **500 XP**.

> [!note]  
> Henchmen and bodyguards do **not** also use a separate reduced XP progression. Their slower advancement comes from receiving smaller XP shares.

## # Followers

## Hirelings and Retainers

Hirelings and ordinary retainers are usually Level 0 characters with **1d4 Hit Dice**.

Examples include:

- Servants.
- Laborers.
- Torchbearers.
- Porters.
- Messengers.
- Grooms.
- Ordinary guards.
- Similar non-adventuring personnel.

They:

- Do not normally accompany PCs into serious danger.
- Do not normally receive Adventure XP.
- Do not normally advance in level.

## Henchmen

Henchmen are loyal adventuring followers such as squires, apprentices, sworn companions, or trusted adventuring retainers.

They are classed characters and generally begin at **Level 1** unless otherwise noted.

Henchmen:

- Receive a **half-share** of Adventure XP when they participate.
- Advance normally using the XP they actually earn.
- Are personally loyal to the character they serve.
- May become important NPC companions in their own right.

## Bodyguards and Household Troops

Bodyguards are sworn retainers maintained through a regent's household, court, temple, guild, or domain.

They generally begin as experienced Level 0 characters with **1d4 Hit Dice**, although veteran guards, knights, champions, or elite retainers may possess class levels.

Examples include:

- Household guards.
- Temple wardens.
- Sworn shields.
- Knights.
- Huscarls.
- Palace guards.
- Domain agents.

Only bodyguards who actively participate in adventures, battles, or dangerous expeditions receive Adventure XP.

Participating bodyguards normally receive a **quarter-share**.

Bodyguards who gain class levels advance normally using the XP they actually earn.

Their primary loyalty is to the regent, household, institution, or domain they serve.

# Regent XP

Regent XP rewards active rulership, meaningful risk, and consequential use of domain resources.

## RP and GB Awards

A regent receives:

- **5 XP for each RP voluntarily spent to influence the outcome of a contested, risky, or consequential domain action.**
    
- **50 XP for each GB voluntarily spent to influence the outcome of such an action.**
    

This includes meaningful offensive or defensive bidding and counterbidding.

The following do **not** grant Regent XP:

- Base action costs.
    
- Routine maintenance.
    
- Court expenses.
    
- Unit maintenance.
    
- Automatic expenses.
    
- Passive costs.
    
- Routine administration.
    
- Spending that carries no meaningful risk, opposition, or consequence.
    

XP is awarded whether the consequential action succeeds or fails, provided the expenditure genuinely affected the attempt.

Resources spent by a lieutenant on the regent's behalf count only if they came from the regent's domain and the action was consequential.

## Achievement Awards

|**Achievement**|**Typical XP**|**Scope**|
|---|---|---|
|Minor|100-300 XP|Local, political, tactical, or personal consequence|
|Major|500-1,500 XP|Changes borders, political relationships, regional power, or creates lasting consequences|
|Legendary|2,500-5,000 XP|Alters kingdoms, dynasties, bloodlines, or the course of history|

The Referee determines whether an accomplishment qualifies.

Routine administration and automatic success do not normally earn achievement XP.

A simple **Rule Province** action is not automatically an achievement. Successfully ruling a province during famine while rivals undermine the regent and raiders threaten the border might qualify because of the risk and consequences.

Regent XP may be divided among multiple participating regents when appropriate.

## Lieutenants

A regent may begin play with one lieutenant per DP spent.

The lieutenant is a loyal henchman of a class chosen by the player. The Referee secretly determines the lieutenant's level.

## Loyalty

For 1 DP, the regent may raise the loyalty of one province to High.

Each additional DP spent on loyalty raises two more provinces to High loyalty.

## Trade Routes

Each DP buys one legal trade route.

All normal requirements for the route must still be met unless the campaign specifically grants an exception during domain creation.

### Land Trade Routes

A land trade route assumes that any required starting road already exists.

### Sea Trade Routes

A sea trade route costs 1 DP.

Ships are not included in this cost.

The regent may use owned ships, leased ships, guild shipping, allied vessels, or local merchant traffic as the Referee permits.

A coastal route of no more than five coastal waterway regions may rely on keelboats, fishing boats, barges, and local traffic without requiring dedicated trade ships.

A longer route, or one crossing open water, requires enough available cargo capacity to transport the route's full GB value.

If available shipping cannot carry the full route value, the route earns only the amount that can actually be transported that domain turn.

## Treasury

One DP adds **5 GB** to the regent's starting treasury.

There is no limit to the number of DP that may be spent on treasury unless the Referee imposes one.
# Combat

## Combat Sequence

1. **Check for Surprise.**  
    The Referee determines whether either or both sides might be surprised. Any side subject to surprise rolls normally.
    
2. **Declare Spells.**  
    Spellcasters who intend to cast this round declare their spells. A surprised side cannot declare spells during the surprise round.
    
3. **Determine Initiative.**  
    Each side rolls 1d6. The higher result wins initiative. On the first round, if only one side is surprised, the other side acts first automatically. Ties are simultaneous.
    
4. **Movement and Missile Fire.**  
    Combatants on the winning side may **move or fire a missile weapon**, followed by the losing side.
    
5. **Melee Combat and Spells.**  
    Combatants on the winning side make melee attacks or cast declared spells, followed by the losing side.
    
6. **End the Round.**  
    Return to Step 2 and continue until combat ends.
    

## Declared Spells and Interruption

A spell must be declared before initiative and is cast during the caster's side of the Melee Combat and Spells phase.

If the caster becomes unable to cast before that phase because an enemy engages them in melee, the spell is **not cast and is not lost from memory**.

If the caster suffers damage or another effect that specifically disrupts spellcasting after declaring the spell but before casting it, the spell is **lost**, unless another campaign rule says otherwise.

A caster whose declared spell cannot be cast may take no other spellcasting action that round, but may defend normally and take any incidental action the Referee considers reasonable.

## Critical Hits

A natural 20 on an attack roll is a critical hit.

|**Character Level**|**Critical Damage**|
|---|---|
|1-5|Maximum weapon damage + 1d4|
|6-10|Maximum weapon damage + 1d6|
|11-15|Maximum weapon damage + 1d8|
|16-20|Maximum weapon damage + 1d10|

**Maximum weapon damage** means the maximum result of the weapon's normal damage dice.

Apply Strength, magic, specialization, and other static damage modifiers **once**, after determining the critical damage.

Example: A fighter with +2 Strength damage scores a critical hit with a longsword (1d8) at 4th level. The damage is:

**8 + 1d4 + 2**

## Critical Mishaps

When a character rolls a natural 1 on an attack roll, roll 1d20 again.

- **If the second roll is not a 1:** The character acts after all other combatants during the next round, regardless of which side wins initiative.
    
- **If the second roll is also a 1:** The character drops the weapon being used. During the next round, the character's action is spent recovering that weapon or drawing another. The character performs that action after all other combatants.
    

If the attack did not use a held weapon, the Referee substitutes an appropriate minor mishap instead of dropping a weapon.
